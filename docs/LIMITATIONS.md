# Limitations and requirement status

## Current Phase 4 status

The newer [Phase 4 validation](PHASE4_VALIDATION.md) supersedes implementation limitations below about the two-state inference, static forest UAV and offline-only warning engines. Four-state per-trail inference, cost-sensitive OBSERVE, live expected-loss/VI/frozen-Q engines, risk routing and Nav2 approval/replacement are implemented and demonstrated. The paired comparison separates informed and unaware crews while holding robot hazard observations fixed. Q-learning convergence, the occasional installed-bridge shutdown assertion, larger multi-seed evaluation and validated human/fire/flight models remain limitations. No human-subject efficacy claim is made.

## Historical baseline evidence (before Phases 2–4)

This is an operational Gazebo/ROS 2 research prototype with measured integration results. It is not a completed validation of the proposed HRI benefit. The scope below separates executable functionality, recorded tests, and work still needed for the full proposal.

The [20-run comparison](../results/comparison.json) contains two seeds × five policies × two worlds, each observed for 70 simulated seconds. Every run reached safety with zero measured hazard exposure. B1 issued no warnings; B2, B3, B4 and the oracle each issued one warning per run. This matrix therefore demonstrates neither a safety improvement nor fewer interruptions for B4. Undefined detection lead times are retained as null. These are legitimate outcomes, not missing successes to be filled in.

The newer industrial operator adviser is separate from those responder trials. It consumes actual waypoint requests, Nav2 plans and observed hazards; it publishes operator belief, route advice and warnings, accepts explicit acknowledgment, and leaves motion to Nav2. Its [successful integration check](../results/operator-integration.json) records an explicit warning about observed fire, confirmed goal cancellation, acknowledgment setting modeled awareness to 1, and arrival within the tolerance of a revised `(2, 0)` waypoint. The [first failed attempt](../results/operator-first-failure.json) remains archived; the later pass does not erase that failure. Do not attribute the 20 responder trials to this adviser: batch runs use `nav2:=false` and do not simulate operator decision-making. This integration check is not an operator study. The adviser currently uses B4 only, treats a shortest graph route as a hypothesis about an endpoint request, and treats acknowledgment as certain awareness until restart. Its Bayesian updates do not currently apply the responder HMM's timed acquisition/forgetting transitions. No true private operator belief is available for accuracy scoring.

The UAV is a static, colliding quadrotor-shaped lookout with live Gazebo pose and downward sensors. It has no autonomous flight, battery or aerodynamic model. A separate mission monitor publishes `/hri/uav_status` for both scenario launches, using actual pose and sensor timestamps to distinguish observing, waiting for a sensor, and stale telemetry. Its assigned mission is a stationary lookout, not autonomous flight.

Important modeling assumptions are:

- Logical cameras provide exact configured model identities within their frustum, without visual occlusion, smoke degradation, classification errors or sensor false positives. RGB and lidar are real independent Gazebo outputs; hazard recognition is not performed on RGB pixels.
- A static semantic catalog associates incident identities with route edges, kinds and exposure radii. Incident existence is activated only by sensor detection, and observed positions are reconstructed from camera/model poses. Processes load a shared configuration; this is a code-level information boundary, not enforced access isolation from configuration metadata.
- Fallen-tree injection creates an already-fallen colliding log. Fire spread inserts additional Gazebo entities on a schedule. Smoke/gas are visual volumes; no combustion, heat transfer, plume dynamics, injury or toxicology is modeled.
- Human position, headings and motion come from Gazebo. Human route intention is deliberately broadcast, and the HRI inference receives this declaration. Exact team tracking and intent availability are favorable assumptions that require validation before transfer to people.
- Exposure measures time within configured incident radii. Entrapment means ten seconds of near-stationary motion inside a hazard radius; it does not establish that all escape paths are physically unavailable. Belief samples over time are correlated. Warning precision is message-level while recall is incident-level.
- Trust, compliance, attention rates, transition probabilities and warning costs are not calibrated against firefighter data. Default local visibility allows the responder to avoid the initial obstacle even without proactive warnings.

Reproducibility means a documented software environment, fixed scenario settings and seeded decision streams, not bit-identical asynchronous physics across machines. The [runtime manifest](../results/runtime_manifest.json) records installed packages and hashes the terrain OBJ. The original per-batch manifests omit OBJ files, although they hash the generator and SDF configuration; the current runner includes mesh and image assets in new manifests. The current experiment runner requires advancing physical pose, behavior and sensor evidence, successful creation of every incident due within the observed simulation window, and detection of the first scheduled incident. It does not require detection of every later incident, so later detection coverage must be audited separately. Preserve original logs and manifests rather than rewriting them after source changes.

The remaining research work is to expand the small sensitivity check into a prespecified larger paired-seed study, evaluate integrated advanced policies if required, conduct a separate operator-adviser study beyond the completed integration check, and validate perception and human-response assumptions. None of the current evidence supports operational deployment or a general claim of improved firefighter safety.

Phase 1 foundation updates, measured stop/collision/waypoint checks, exact commands and limitations are recorded in [PHASE1_VALIDATION.md](PHASE1_VALIDATION.md).

Phase 2 adds optional A–F forest scenarios with scripted UAV motion, wind/fuel fire growth, route costs and replay controls. The original baseline UAV remains stationary when no forest case is selected. These new capabilities and their separate limits/evidence are documented in [FOREST_SCENARIO.md](FOREST_SCENARIO.md) and [PHASE2_VALIDATION.md](PHASE2_VALIDATION.md); they do not alter the provenance of earlier baseline experiments.

Phase 3 supersedes the shared-configuration inference boundary described above for the **default** model: the new node receives an allowlisted public graph, geometric sensor detections, public intent, actual odometry, and warnings. It does not load the scenario or read private truth. The legacy model remains explicitly selectable with `tom:=false`. The new four-state filter is factorized with a MAP context approximation; action ambiguity, observation blind spots, uncalibrated compliance, correlated time samples and rationality misspecification remain limitations. Full definitions and the limited scope of the six demonstration conditions are in [BAYESIAN_TOM.md](BAYESIAN_TOM.md).
